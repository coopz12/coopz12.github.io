Troposphere

0 TO 12 KM

All weather lives in this first thin band. Clouds, storms, wind and rain are packed into the lowest slice of the sky.

Airliners cruise at 10 to 12 km, near the ceiling of this layer, where the air runs smooth.

Stratosphere

12 TO 50 KM

The ozone layer sits here and absorbs ultraviolet sunlight. The air is dry, clear and very thin, with almost no weather at all.

Concorde cruised at about 18 km, higher than any airliner flying today.

Mesosphere

50 TO 85 KM

The coldest layer of the atmosphere. The air is too thin to feel, but still thick enough to burn up meteors falling through it.

Near its top the temperature drops to about -90°C, the coldest in the whole atmosphere.

Thermosphere

85 TO 100 KM

Here the air thins to almost nothing, a few stray atoms at a time. Auroras flare in this band, lit by charged particles from the Sun.

The International Space Station orbits inside this layer, at about 400 km up.
